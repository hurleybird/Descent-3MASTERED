# Dynamic shadows (experimental)

On the playable renderer branch, F2 → Video → **Dynamic shadows (per-pixel)** enables object and room shadows. Per-pixel lighting must also be on. Shadows default off; the command-line overrides are `-dynamic-shadows` and `-no-dynamic-shadows`.

Version 1 picked a light by its distance from the eye, then put the room walls and the actors into the same map. In save 007 its chosen pulsing light made no visible difference at frame 50, even with the launcher's SSAA, MSAA, AO and bloom. The staged flare test wasn't representative. Putting room faces into their own shadow map also allowed their edges to be marked as blockers.

One slot is reserved for a live emitter: a headlight, flare, light-emitting weapon bolt, or other dynamic light. The shadow budget does not limit how many lights illuminate the scene. Four other slots can represent light-emitting room faces. Their locations, colors, areas and intensities come from level data. They are not additional additive lights. On lightmapped receivers we estimate the removable direct contribution using the game's own destructible-light radius; overlapping estimates share a bounded portion of the bake. Source separation is approximate: the original lightmap does not retain individual lights. The lightmap itself is never rewritten.

Version 2 dropped lights if an estimated shadow position fell outside the current camera. In the mirror-save chase turn, that evicted a fixed rod-tip light between frames 67 and 68 while its shadow was still on the ceiling. Version 3 budgets light/caster pairs by physical position, reach, and proximity to the player, independently of which way any camera points. Casters use a fixed model detail for the light pass. When physical movement really does change the budget, an existing light fades out before its slot is reused; a light that no longer exists is not kept alive.

Version 4 adds opaque room faces to the **live emitter only**. Open portals and alpha-cutout faces do not become solid sheets. The source is eligible in an empty room without a nearby model; the map uses rooms reached by the light, not rooms visible to the eye. The four fixed lamps still use only opaque rigid model parts (including offscreen models and the exterior ship hull); they do not double-shadow the baked architecture. With shadows enabled, the headlight is mounted on the port bow and aimed back toward the reticle at midrange. Illumination, specular and occlusion share that one position. With shadows off, its original position and illumination are unchanged.

Room receivers can be in the same map they sample. Each live-light tap tests the actual shadow-texel center against the receiver triangle plane, with a tolerance measured normal to that plane. It must also be closer to the light than the receiver in radial distance: extrapolating only the plane at grazing incidence can make a distant wall shadow a flare's contact surface. The geometric plane comes from camera-relative positions before fragment discards using the otherwise unused vertex-light components; no additional interpolator or texture is required. The light views share the same 384×384×30 R32F array (~17 MiB) and one depth buffer as before. Only occupied directions overlapping the beam/filter are drawn.

Limits: authored lamps currently affect lightmapped surfaces in their own room. Solid architecture and terrain receive live shadows; terrain and cutout fences do not cast. There is no outdoor sun pass, model-on-model shadowing or volumetric shadowing.

Room-shadow regression: load save 005 (stalactite) with `-dynamic-shadows -capture-headlight` and compare to the same frame with `-capture-model-casters-only`. The control changes only room casting, not the light or camera. A pointed shadow should appear on the wall to the right of the rock. In the same save, `-capture-no-headlight -capture-fire-flare` separates the wall-contact flare from the headlight. Save 004 (training tunnel) checks for false triangle outlines. See `tools/renderer-scene-tests/check_room_shadows.py` for the paired-image checks.

Gameplay repro: load mirror save 006, fire a flare, then turn without leaving the room. For the repeatable chase-camera comparison, use `-capture-frame 65 -capture-dt 0.016666667 -capture-seed 12345 -capture-fire-flare -capture-heading -capture-heading-from 12 -capture-chase-camera`. It uses the existing ship, level lighting, flare gun and chase camera. Compare with shadows off. To isolate the ship in cockpit view use `-capture-no-pilot-shadow`. To exercise the enabled receiver shader and all light selection while guaranteeing no blockers, use `-capture-shadow-clear-only`. These controls only operate during automated capture.
